*How much confidence do we actually have?*

Read the pyramid bottom-up. A claim is only as trustworthy as the layer holding it up: validators beneath
measurements beneath claims. A claim with a validator under it is proven; a claim with only a measurement is
grounded; a claim with neither is an assertion — and an asserted reality-claim with no backing of any kind is
the confidence hole to close before you trust it.

This card comes from the book's own evidence apparatus. The point is not to count evidence but to see where
each claim sits — how far up the pyramid its support reaches — so an operator knows which claims to lean on
and which are still bare.

### The three layers

- **Claims (top).** The claim universe — spine claims, theory nodes, and discussion-claims — every
  reality-asserting sentence the book stands behind. This is the population the confidence question is asked
  over.
- **Measurements (middle).** The metric-to-claim ledger: each datum bound to the claim it supports, carrying
  its observable, its data source with provenance, and its honest limitation. A measurement lifts a claim off
  the page and onto the ground.
- **Validators (bottom).** The generative-validation coverage oracles — grammar coverage and model-claim
  coverage rising toward full exercise — plus the correctness and conformance validators. A validator is the
  strongest footing: it re-checks the claim on every run.

```
  EVIDENCE QUALITY                      "How much confidence do we have?"
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
                        /  Claims  \            asserted
                      /--------------\            ^
                    /   Measurements   \          |  confidence rises
                  /----------------------\        |  with the layer
                /       Validators         \      |  beneath
              /------------------------------\   proven
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Confidence hole:  soapbox band (claims with no backing) → must be 0
  Validator reach:  coverage oracles exercised toward full
```

The soapbox band — reality-claims asserted with zero backing — is the confidence gauge, and it is gated to
empty; a non-empty band fails the book's own substantiation check. The second anchor is the coverage-oracle
reading, which says how completely the validators exercise what they guard.

### What this projects

The book's substantiation apparatus — the claim universe with its three evidence legs nested under each
claim, projected as Claims, Measurements, Validators; the metric-to-claim data ledger (the middle tier); and
generative validation ([Chapter 4.6](4.6-generative-validation.html)) as the base tier. The soapbox gate is
the apparatus's own no-backing check, rendered as the card's confidence gauge.
