*Is the environment becoming more valuable, or are we just moving faster?*

Not a single metric — an operator's assessment across five indicators. Green means recurring failures are
disappearing into reused mechanisms while per-change attention falls; Red means the apparatus has grown past
its fit, or holds capital that outlived it. This is the deck's endpoint: engineering capital is the book's
argument made operational — the accumulated governance that lowers the reasoning a future change demands.

### The five indicators

1. **Support ratio.** Support-apparatus lines over production lines — a structural stock, settling around 3x
   at maturity. One indicator, not the headline.
2. **Mechanism reuse.** Reused across sites, not re-invented? Soft-gap; proxy is the catalogue's known-uses count.
3. **Recurring failures.** Are the same classes disappearing? Soft-gap — a failure-class construct, no count.
4. **Manual judgment.** Is per-change attention falling as classes convert? Reads oversight amortization.
5. **Drift stability.** Do the sync gates hold steady green? Reads Model-sync efficacy.

### Reading the banner

- **Green — growing.** Failures disappearing; mechanisms reused; reviews shrinking per change; gates steady.
- **Yellow — bureaucracy risk.** Duplicate mechanisms; stale constraints; support ratio climbing past its fit
  with no capability payoff.
- **Red — decaying.** Retired mechanisms never removed; the apparatus pushed past its point of fit; capital
  that outlived its fit and is no longer subtractively reconciled.

### What this projects

The compounding-governance proposition — as recurring judgment converts into durable mechanisms, an
increasing fraction of future effort extends capability rather than re-solving understood problems, for as
long as the capital stays fit — plus the *compounding* concept and the governance-adaptation to
capability-amplification composition. The proposition's caveat sets the Red band: returns attach to capital
quality, not apparatus quantity.
