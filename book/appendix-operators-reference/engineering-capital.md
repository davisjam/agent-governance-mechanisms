*Is accumulated governance reducing future reasoning cost?*

Not a single metric — an operator's read across five indicators. Green means recurring failures are
disappearing into reused mechanisms while per-change attention falls; Red means the apparatus outgrew its fit,
or holds capital that outlived it. This is the deck's centerpiece: the five indicators are evidence for one
proposition — that accumulated governance lowers the reasoning a future change demands.

Healthy direction: support apparatus leads production and settles to a stable multiple; recurring classes keep
disappearing while per-change attention falls.
*DocAble reference:* support ratio ~3× at maturity; controls 0 → 747 lints, 0 → 102 gates — one observed run,
not a target.

### The five indicators

1. **Support ratio.** Support-apparatus lines over production lines — a structural stock. Green is a stable multiple, not a climbing one.
2. **Mechanism reuse.** Reused across sites, not re-invented. Soft-gap; proxy is the catalogue's known-uses count.
3. **Recurring failures.** The same classes disappearing. Soft-gap — a failure-class construct, no count.
4. **Manual judgment.** Per-change attention falling as classes convert. Reads oversight amortization.
5. **Drift stability.** The sync gates holding steady green. Reads Model-sync efficacy.

### Reading the banner

- **Green — growing.** Failures disappearing; mechanisms reused; reviews shrinking per change; gates steady.
- **Yellow — bureaucracy risk.** Duplicate mechanisms; stale constraints; support ratio climbing with no capability payoff.
- **Red — decaying.** Retired mechanisms never removed; the apparatus pushed past its fit; capital no longer subtractively reconciled.

### What this projects

The compounding-governance proposition — as recurring judgment converts into durable mechanisms, an increasing
fraction of future effort extends capability rather than re-solving understood problems, as long as the capital
stays fit. The proposition's caveat sets the Red band: returns attach to capital quality, not apparatus quantity.
