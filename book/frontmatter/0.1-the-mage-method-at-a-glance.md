# The MAGE Method at a Glance

MAGE is one method with two theses and a conversion that grows an environment around them. To help you understand the full argument, [ref:mage-method] shows the whole of it on a single page and how the parts relate to each other — the vocabulary this book builds, laid out in the order the method runs.

<!-- label: mage-method -->
<!-- figure: assets/mage-method.svg | The whole method on one page. A coding agent — a reasoning engine paired with a printer — generates code through a fleet, and left ungoverned that fleet drifts into churn. Two theses answer it: the Modeling Thesis binds intent to structured models the fleet reasons through, and the Alignment Thesis holds each change to intent with mechanisms the environment enforces. Together they fill a governed environment, bootstrapped up front from what you already know, then grown failure by failure through governance conversion. What the loop produces, once the environment holds, is trustworthy software at velocity. -->

The method above runs on a theory the closing chapters develop in full; here it is in two compact forms you can return to. [ref:mage-theory-card] states it as a one-page reference card — the problem, the two theses, the conversion, and what compounds. [ref:mage-theory-dynamics] draws the same theory as two intersecting loops, the companion dynamics view to the method architecture above.
